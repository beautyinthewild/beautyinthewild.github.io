Beauty in the Wild

- A quest for citizen science in the Atlantic Sea -

Beauty in the Wild is a resource hub for anyone interested in marine biology, marine conservation, and marine affairs. It is largely focused on sea turtle conservation and research in the Atlantic Sea. If you want to live and work on a tropical island, you’ve come to the right place!

Living on a island has its triumphs and challenges for locals and newcomers. Imagine being spoiled with blissful sunrises and sunsets, fresh picked fruits, pocket secret beaches, quiet spaces, and marine life. You’ll see that living without extra furniture and materials will make you happier. Living simply is key to longevity, mental health and happiness. Bare in mind that an island’s pace of life runs slower, shortage is common among goods and produce, and power outages are frequent. You will be naturally in tuned with your surroundings, and gardening or farming will become a part of your life. Small shops and gas stations close early, so its wise to plan your day accordingly, especially if you need to visit the post office.

Be open to building rapports with locals, and integrate yourself in community activities, festivities, and outreach. Knowing people will enrich your experience and benefit you when you need ideas, opinions or a service. If you are leading a role in a villa/village/town be prepared to be on call (especially for sea turtle season) and demonstrate a sharp field acumen. Once you’ve settled in and have a familiarity of your surroundings, you’ll become adroit at dealing with shifts in field schedules or protocol changes, and working with people of different personalities.

If you are looking to enriched yourself with culture and community conservation, ask yourself these questions: Will I be comfortable to handle situations when there is no electricity or water? Am I open to living among diverse cultures such as; Latin, African, Caribbean, Portuguese or French? Are there living foods to nourish me? I hope so because putting yourself out of your comfort zone is our only true growth. Be mindful and respectful to others no matter what situations arise with your teammates in the field or in living quarters. Don’t be afraid to express your feelings and concerns.

After working many seasons with marine turtles, I ventured a week to the Ann Wigmore Natural Health Institute in Puerto Rico, to learn how to nourish and heal my body with living foods. Working with marine life is the dream, as we uncover more information about the ocean, and how species are changing and coping with climate change. It also means working long hours in the field, putting different kinds of stress on your body and sometimes eating low nutrient foods that makes you feel crabby. All of this can be changed by adding green smoothies, fresh fruits and produce and fresh-squeezed juices in your meals to keep your energy afloat!

Finding your niche is the field is challenging when your trying to figure out the next step. Personally, I think creating a vision board is the best tool to visualize your actions and goals. I promise you. Make it a tradition to do it once a year. Through this blog, I hope it inspires you to explore the terrestial and marine environment around the world. Discover what makes your heart spark and let that take over you. If that means becoming your own entrepreneur or consultant, do it and never stop growing.
